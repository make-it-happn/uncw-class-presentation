02

Lesson Learned

Be careful who you
get into business with

  • Don't go 50/50 with someone you don't fully trust
  • Watch for red flags — if the values don't align, that's a data point
  • Getting him out was painful. The real question: do they share your values?
03

Team Building

You should be ecstatic
about every hire

  • If you're thinking "I guess they're good enough" - that's not good enough
  • Never settle. The wrong hire at an early stage can set you back months
  • In interviews, fully understand what someone is telling you - don't just nod and move on
  • If you don't understand, say so: "That sounds great, but can you explain what you mean?"
  • Anyone truly smart can explain themselves clearly. If they can't, that's a signal
  • If you leave an interview still wondering - that's on you for not asking

The Interview Process

Design it. Follow it.
Every time.

1
Phone Screen
First impression, energy, communication
→
2
1-on-1 Interview
Deep dive, values alignment, honesty
→
3
Team Setting
How do they treat everyone in the room?
→
4
Coffee or Meal
How do they treat the waiter?

Keep the process the same for every candidate. Multiple steps, different settings. See the real person - not just a good day.

04

Customer Obsession

How bad do you
want it?

  • At Exit Intel we had no choice - we were in the trenches with customers every day
  • When I started Amped I drifted - I hired a salesperson and stepped back too early
  • At that pivotal young stage, I couldn't let someone else lead - I had to roll my sleeves up
  • Go talk to 20 customers. It's stereotypical advice because it absolutely works
  • Know what good looks like firsthand - then build the product around that
05

Just Go

You don't need
permission.

  • Register the LLC and charge someone money for something
  • Stripe, Lovable, Cursor, Claude Code — the tools are incredible right now
  • Be unbelievably curious: What is possible? How could I do that?
  • Build something, test it, see if people will pay
06

Learn From Others

Seek out people who've
been there

  • Find entrepreneurs who've actually done it - and spend time with them
  • When you're early, there's almost zero skepticism - they don't think you're selling them anything
  • Most good entrepreneurs will absolutely grab coffee and chat with you
  • Ask them their stories. If you have an idea, get their input. Learn from their experience
  • Let yourself get inspired - that energy is contagious and it compounds

Values

Authentically help.
and integrity is everything.

  • If you are truly trying to help your customers succeed, they will see it
  • They'll tell their friends. That's how you grow - high trust, real results
  • Integrity is the foundation. Do not compromise it. Ever
  • It applies to everything - how you treat customers, partners, your team, yourself
07

Level Up

Optimize your
routine

  • How are you leveling up personally? You've got to continually be doing that
  • Is your daily schedule optimized for highest impact?
  • Too much time on social media? Get off of it
  • Invest in real-life relationships - that's where the real returns are
08

Leadership

Lead the mission.
Learn to sell.

Get people fired up. Set the vision. Inspire them to come along on the journey. And learn how to sell — that's the most important thing you can learn as an entrepreneur.

Radical Candor · Kim Scott

Being "nice" by avoiding hard conversations isn't caring - it's failing them. If someone didn't do a good job, address it clearly. You don't tiptoe. You just say it with care.

09

Systems

Business is all
about systems

  • Every business is a function of the systems you set up to scale
  • Three types: the entrepreneur, the manager, and the technician
  • It cannot be about you — build it so it runs without you
  • Once you get some scale, look at EOS for solid foundations

Build With AI

You have a workshop
in your laptop

  • The tools available right now are insane - use them
  • My stack: Cursor + Claude Code (Opus) for building, Supabase for backend, Vercel to deploy
  • I run AI agents that handle real work - syncing meetings, managing finances, drafting emails

Persistence

It will not be easy
keep going, keep learning

  • You will fail. A lot. Most ideas won't work - that's normal
  • Monday you'll feel unstoppable. Tuesday you'll want to quit. Wednesday you get back up
  • Every successful founder has a graveyard of failed attempts behind them
  • The people who win are the ones crazy enough to keep going
